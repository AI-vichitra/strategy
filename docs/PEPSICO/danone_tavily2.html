What Are the Key Customer Segments of Danone? [...] 14. What Is the History of Danone? Danone traces its origins to 1919, when Isaac Carasso began selling yogurt in Barcelona. The brand name came from the Catalan nickname of his son Daniel. In 1929, Daniel Carasso expanded the business into France, laying the foundation for what became one of Europe’s best-known food groups. The modern corporation emerged through a series of combinations. Gervais merged with Danone in 1967, and BSN later combined with Gervais Danone in 1973. BSN eventually adopted the Danone name in 1994, reflecting the growing strategic centrality of the brand and the company’s consumer-food identity.

It was the plight of Spanish children in particular that inspired Isaac Carasso, a Greek immigrant living in Barcelona, to take interest in the work of Élie Metchnikoff (also spelled “Ilya” Metchnikoff). Metchnikoff was a Nobel Prize winner and considered by many to be “the father of innate immunity.” It was this interest in Metchnikoff’s work that led Carasso to advocate for the health benefits of yogurt. Carasso believed that this traditional fermented dairy product of the Balkans would be able to tackle the widespread nutritional problems that he witnessed in his adopted home.
